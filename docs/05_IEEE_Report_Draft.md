## Abstract
Mental-health text classifiers are usually reported with a single overall score, which can hide differences across age groups and languages. We present an open, reproducible Python framework that (i) analyses label and text patterns across age bands, (ii) analyses multilingual and code-mixed text (English, Hindi, Marathi, Hinglish, Romanized Marathi), (iii) examines their combination, (iv) compares three baseline classifiers overall and per group with confidence intervals and fairness gaps, and (v) uses generative AI to produce guard-railed, number-verified summaries and reviewable multilingual awareness content. Privacy is protected by PII scrubbing, author-grouped validation, and sending only aggregated statistics to any external model. We validate the pipeline on synthetic data and on open proxy-labelled corpora, and describe a consented survey protocol for age- and language-labelled data. `[TODO: add one sentence with the main real-data finding after Track C / Reddit runs.]` The framework is a research tool, not a diagnostic system.

**Keywords:** mental health, text classification, age groups, multilingual NLP, code-mixed text, fairness, explainability, generative AI.

## I. Introduction
Text from social media and surveys is widely studied for depression-related signals. Most work reports aggregate accuracy, yet language, culture and age change how people express distress, so aggregate scores may mask subgroup failures. India adds code-mixing (e.g., Hinglish, Romanized Marathi) that standard English pipelines handle poorly. Real datasets that jointly provide text, a mental-health label, age and language are rare, so careful dataset handling is itself a contribution.

**Objectives.** O1 age-wise text patterns; O2 age-based risk-category analysis; O3 multilingual and code-mixed analysis; O4 age x language analysis; O5 group-wise model comparison; O6 generative-AI age-wise insights; O7 generative-AI multilingual awareness content.

**Contributions.** (1) A reproducible pipeline with leakage-safe, author-grouped evaluation; (2) subgroup reporting that suppresses under-powered groups and reports uncertainty; (3) conservative code-mixed normalization that preserves original text; (4) a number-verification guardrail for generated summaries; (5) a consent and ethics protocol for collecting age- and language-labelled survey data.

## IV. Method
### A. Preprocessing
Raw text is preserved in `text_original`. URLs, e-mails, @handles and phone numbers are replaced by placeholders. Whitespace and Unicode (NFC) are normalized; case, negations and pronouns are kept. Rows with missing text or label, exact duplicates, and texts with conflicting labels are removed and counted. Ages outside 13-100 or non-numeric are set to missing, never imputed. Age bands: 13-17, 18-25, 26-35, 36-45, 46+. Author identifiers are hashed.

### B. Language identification and code-mixed normalization
A rule-based identifier combines Unicode script ratios with marker-word lists to output en, hi, mr, hinglish, roman_mr, code_mixed, deva_unknown or unknown; it is used as a support signal when no language label is provided. Conservative normalization collapses letter elongation and maps a small set of well-known spelling variants (e.g., *bohot*, *bahot* to *bahut*) in Romanized text only, keeping the original.

### C. Statistical analysis (O1-O4)
Label-by-group tables report counts, percentages and 95% Wilson intervals [7]. Association between label and age band or language is tested with chi-square and Cramer's V; text features (word count, first-person pronoun rate) with Kruskal-Wallis and epsilon-squared; p-values are corrected with Benjamini-Hochberg [8]. Distinctive terms per group use the log-odds ratio with an informative prior z-score [9]. Groups with fewer than 30 posts are flagged and hidden in cross-group figures.

### D. Models and evaluation (O5)
Features: word 1-2-gram and character 2-5-gram TF-IDF. Models: logistic regression, linear SVM and complement naive Bayes (scikit-learn [10]). Evaluation uses 5-fold stratified cross-validation with all posts of one author in the same fold, repeated for three seeds; vectorizers are fit inside training folds only. Metrics: macro-F1 (primary), accuracy, macro precision/recall, MCC. Per-group macro-F1 is reported only for groups with at least 100 posts, with bootstrap 95% intervals (200 resamples) and a gap statistic (max minus min macro-F1) per group type.

### E. Explainability
Largest logistic-regression word coefficients per label are reported as descriptions of model behaviour, not causes.

### F. Generative AI (O6, O7)
*Insights (O6):* aggregated statistics (groups below the minimum size suppressed) are converted into a short summary. The default offline mode is template-based; an optional online mode calls an OpenAI-compatible model. A guardrail extracts every number in the output and requires a match to the input statistics (at the number's precision) and rejects banned clinical phrases; failures fall back to the template. *Awareness content (O7):* general, non-clinical text in English, Hindi and Marathi is tailored to four age tones. Each item carries a disclaimer, a helpline placeholder and the status `PENDING_HUMAN_REVIEW`; native-speaker and qualified reviewer approval is required. No raw posts are sent to any external service.

### G. Implementation
Python 3.14, pandas, scikit-learn, SciPy, matplotlib/seaborn, Streamlit; configuration in `config/config.yaml`; 31+ automated tests; command-line pipeline and dashboard.

## V. Ethics and Responsible Use
The system analyses text patterns and does not diagnose. Age is never inferred from writing. Labels are proxies unless stated. Survey data collection follows the consent pack (`docs/04`), requires institutional approval, excludes minors unless guardian procedures are approved, and displays support information. Raw examples are not published. Bias risks: platform demographics, language and dialect variation, label noise, and subgroup imbalance.

## VIII. Limitations and Threats to Validity
Proxy labels; unverified self-reported age and self-diagnosis; Reddit population bias; small subgroups, especially for age x language; rule-based language identification; synthetic data only validates code; no clinical validation; generated content requires human review; results may not generalize across platforms or regions.

## IX. Conclusion and Future Work
We provide a reproducible framework for age- and language-aware evaluation of mental-health text models with privacy and uncertainty safeguards. Future work: collect Track C data at scale, add transformer baselines (XLM-R, MuRIL), calibration analysis, stability of explanations across groups, and native-speaker evaluation of generated content.
